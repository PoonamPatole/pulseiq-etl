# Attendance & Leave Analysis — Insights

## Finding 1: Attendance varies by department
Department D08 has the lowest attendance rate at **90.6%**, while D03 and D04 have the highest at **94.8%**.

**Meaning:** D08 may need closer review to understand its attendance patterns.

**HR action:** Review D08's staffing, leave patterns, and scheduling before deciding on any action.

## Finding 2: Attendance improved in September 2026
Monthly attendance was lowest in **May 2026 at 92.6%** and reached **95.0% in September 2026**. The three-month rolling average also rose to **94.29%** in September.

**Meaning:** Attendance improved toward the end of the observed period, but the reason is not established.

**HR action:** Compare future months and check whether staffing or scheduling changes explain the improvement.

## Finding 3: Late arrivals increased toward the end of the period
Recorded late arrivals rose to **618 in September 2026**, the highest monthly count in the results.

**Meaning:** This could reflect punctuality issues, changes in workforce size, or differences in recorded attendance.

**HR action:** Review late arrivals by department and compare rates, not only counts, before taking action.

## Limitations
The data may be synthetic. These patterns do not establish causes or prove real employee behaviour. Department sizes differ, and late-arrival counts are not adjusted for headcount. The anomaly sample is model-generated and should be treated as a signal for investigation, not proof of a problem. The December attendance pattern should be checked against other years or independent real-world data before calling it seasonal.

**Anomaly limitation:**
Flagged anomaly counts and periods are displayed separately from the attendance trend line. Individual anomaly markers are not overlaid on the trend chart. The anomaly flags are signals for investigation, not proof of an actual attendance problem.
